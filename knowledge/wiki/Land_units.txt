# Nomenclature [verified for 1.9]

The terms used in the game (e.g. regiment, brigade) have historically had different meanings in the militaries of different countries. This section explains the significance of these terms as they are defined for this game.

## Division
(Main article: Division)
Armies are composed of divisions, which is the smallest unit a player can directly control.

## Battalion
Each division consists of 1-25 battalions - also sometimes called line battalions. This is the main fighting force, and the primary source of most statistics. The primary drain of manpower and equipment.

## Battalion group and regiment
All battalions belong to a specific classification or group, such as "Infantry Battalion", "Mobile Battalion" and "Armoured Battalion".

Regiments are the "columns" in the template designer. Battalions from different groups must be in different regiments (columns). There is no other effect of regiments. And, as there are 5 regiments/columns but only 3 groups, it is never an issue. There are many other reasons not to use all 25 battalion slots and/or mix more than 1-2 battalion Groups anyway.

## Support company
In addition to the battalions, a division may have up to 5 support companies. These often provide bonuses that cannot be otherwise obtained. Support companies can be roughly put into 4 unofficial groups:
- Artillery, which provides limited artillery tree capabilities for divisions that cannot afford to bring mainline artillery battalions along.
- Basic support, which only needs support equipment
- Motorized support, which needs support Equipment and some motorized
- Recon, which can be cavalry (infantry weapons, support), motorized (infantry weapons, support, and motorized), armoured car, or light tank

# Statistics [verified for 1.9]

Based on the Battalions, each division has a number of values. The calculation of the final values ranges from simple Addition (Summation), Averages, and Worst to "it is complicated" (Armour and Piercing).

Do keep in mind that some values are also defined by the actual fielded equipment. The Division designer will simply assume the "best" equipment is used. But between equipment loss and capture as well as limited production relative to casualties, often much older gear might be pulled out of storage. While that will result in every soldier having something to fight with, it will drastically reduce the values. And of course for some divisions, intentional limiting of the usable equipment might be advisable, especially off-map garrison divisions where most equipment stats do not matter.

Most of these stats can be improved through building and using variants and, with No Step Back , using different tank designs. However, be aware that increasing one stat can adversely affect a different stat. Researching land doctrines can also increase stats, as can various technologies and, with Arms Against Tyranny , certain special forces doctrines. Some national focuses and political advisors can also increase the stats of certain unit types.

## Base stats
- Maximum speed. The division travels at the speed of its slowest battalion. Support companies such as scouting units are counted for this; if you chose a slow scouting company the entire division will move at that speed. Some other support companies are also counted for this.
- HP. "HP" is an abbreviation for "hit points." This is the ability to absorb damage and is largely a function of manpower and role. Also referred to as Strength. Division HP represents how many hits or damage the division can take before it is physically destroyed. The more HP a division has, the fewer losses to its manpower and equipment it will take from the same amount of damage. When a division takes damage to its HP, its combat stats are reduced proportionally; for example, a division at half of its maximum HP can only use half of its maximum possible attacks or defences in combat. Tanks and combat support battalions tend to be relatively fragile when it comes to absorbing damage, and it is usually infantry and mobile battalions, especially mechanized , that enable divisions to absorb serious damage without losing as much equipment or manpower. Reference Damage dealing. The HP for the division is the sum of those of all battalions and companies.
- Organization. Known as "morale" in previous games, organization is the readiness of a division to perform combat, the willingness of a unit to either continue attacking or to continue holding the province that they are defending. Once a unit no longer has any organization remaining, it can no longer fight in battles. Infantry and mobile battalions tend to be the primary source of organization in a division, so it is important to buffer lower-organization battalions like tanks and combat support battalions with enough of them to keep the division combat-capable. Moving a division causes organization loss at the rate of -0.2 organization per hour, and organization loss also slows down movement to up to -80%. Organization may also be lost through attrition or lack of supply, see Organization loss and Out of supply penalties). A division without organization cannot operate as it is supposed to; it cannot stay in combat or move properly. The organization for the division is the average of those of all battalions and companies. Battles are usually battles of Organization depletion (reference Damage dealing) and, depending on who the attacker is, reaching 0 has one of two effects:
 - If it is the attacker, then the division will stop attacking and will either stay in, or return to, the region it came from. Any movement progress is lost. It cannot be ordered to attack again until Organization has recovered a decent amount.
 - If it is the defender, then the division will try to flee: leaving the province to the nearest friendly one. If it cannot leave the province, then it will be destroyed.
- Recovery rate. All divisions that are not in combat regenerate Organization at a fixed rate. This stat is additional regeneration on top of that, allowing a division to be better prepared for more combat. A division can suffer from a recovery rate penalty if it lacks supply. Reference Out of supply penalties. The stat for the division is the average of those of all battalions and companies.
- Recon. Contributes to reconnaissance during battle. In any combat the side with a reconnaissance advantage (from divisions and the general) over their enemy has more information about how their adversaries' forces are deployed and are more likely to pick better tactics for the day (reference Combat tactics). Adding a recon support company will give an extra reconnaissance points bonus to the commander in charge of your division during battle. Reference Support Companies technology. The stat for the division is the sum of those of all battalions and companies.
- Suppression. Ability to suppress local resistance (reference Occupation). How well the division will stop the formation of partisan forces and their activities. The stat for the division is the sum of those of all battalions and companies, though because occupation does not involve deployed divisions, in practice it behaves more like the weighted average of the division (weighted by production cost and manpower ).
- Weight. How large or "heavy" the unit is. The higher the number, the more convoys are needed when in naval transfer and the more planes are needed to drop paratroopers. The stat for the division is the sum of those of all battalions and companies.
- Supply use. How much supply the unit consumes per day. Please read Supply on the significance of this. Adding a Logistics support company will reduce your division's daily supply consumption. Reference Support Companies technology. The stat for the division is the sum of those of all battalions and companies.
- Reliability. Reliability is the resistance to military equipment breakdown and subsequent loss. Higher reliability reduces the chance of equipment breakdown and loss from attrition and increases the chance of recovering lost equipment during combat. Reference Equipment recovery and Attrition and accidents. Incorporating a Maintenance support company (reference Support Companies technology) into your division will grant a bonus to each equipment type's reliability. The stat is tracked individually for each type of equipment used by the division.
- Trickleback. Normally, Manpower that is lost in combat just vanishes. While the Manpower pool does regrow to its maximum, this is very slow. Trickleback is a portion of casualties that can be saved and returned to the Manpower pool, conserving manpower. This is primarily provided by the Field Hospital support company, see Support Companies technology. The stat for the division is the sum of those of all battalions and companies.
- Exp. loss. How much experience is lost when a unit suffers casualties. A high negative is better. A Field Hospital support company can reduce experience loss from casualties. Reference Support Companies technology. Note that there is additional XP loss for bringing in reinforcements. The stat for the division is the sum of those of all battalions and companies.

## Combat stats
- Soft attack. How many attacks made each hour against the soft parts of an enemy division. In combat, this stat gives the number of attacks the unit can make against a target with low hardness (such as infantry or cavalry) instead of high hardness, armoured targets (such as tanks and tank variants). The stat for the division is the sum of those of all battalions and companies.
- Hard attack. How many attacks made each hour against the hard parts of an enemy division. In combat, this stat gives the number of attacks the unit can make against a target with high hardness (such as tanks and armor variants). The stat for the division is the sum of those of all battalions and companies.
- Air attack. How much damage a unit can do against enemy airplanes. It can reduce the effectiveness of enemy aircraft attacking your division (capability of shooting down enemy aircraft). High air attack can also help to counter enemy air superiority effects. The stat for the division is the sum of those of all battalions and companies.
- Defence. Known as "defensiveness" in previous games. This stat represents the number of defences the division has when defending a province, that is, how many attacks it can avoid. When a division has no defences left, it is more probable that it is hit when in combat and cannot hold its defensive position as well. Higher defence allows a division to hold the line longer with fewer casualties. The stat for the division is the sum of those of all battalions and companies.
- Breakthrough. Known as "toughness" in previous games. This works like defence, except it applies when the division is attacking a province. This stat represents the number of defences the division has when attacking, that is, how many of the defender's attacks it can avoid. When the division has no defences left, it is more probable that the division will be hit when in combat and cannot sustain the offensive as well. Higher breakthrough allows a division to continue attacking the line more effectively. The stat for the division is the sum of those of all battalions and companies.
- Armour. Decreases the number of soft and hard attacks made against the division if the attacking division's piercing is less than the defending division's armor, by up to % at less than PIERCING_THRESHOLDS piercing. Higher armor than the enemy's piercing will also increase the number of unit attacks in combat and change the organization damage dice roll per attack from 1d4 to 1d6 as the unit can move around the battlefield more freely without getting pinned or damaged. A division's armour is equal to 40% of the highest armor in the division plus 60% of the average armour in the division. Armor is not a property of unit type, only of its equipment.
- Piercing. Represents the ability of a division to pierce armour and pin down armoured forces, restricting the movement. If the value is higher than the enemy's armor value, then the division suffers no penalty to its damage and suffers no additional organization damage. Otherwise, the division deals % damage with at least PIERCING_THRESHOLDS piercing, the division deals % damage with at least PIERCING_THRESHOLDS piercing, and % damage otherwise. A division's piercing is equal to 40% of the highest piercing in the division plus 60% of the average piercing of all battalions and companies in the division.
- Initiative. Higher initiative speeds up battle planning, so 20% initiative, as from a level 1 signals company, speeds up 2%/day planning to 2.4%/day. Higher initiative also increases the chance of a division joining a battle from the reserves, with each 1% initiative increasing reinforce rate by 0.25 The stat for the division is the sum of those of all battalions and companies.
- Entrenchment. Represents the ability of a unit to build temporary defensive structures like trenches and pick favourable defensive positions like hills, to better defend against an enemy attack. Entrenchment level is mentioned in the army selection. Entrenchment affect both attack and defence stats of divisions. Divisions build up entrenchment daily when not engaged in combat and stationary (not moving). Divisions will lose all their entrenchment if they are attacking, with the exception of using the probing attack ability (Reference: Command Power). It provides a massive bonus when defending the province, going as far as an outright force multiplier. The stat for the division is the sum of those of all battalions and companies.
- Combat width. Represents the room that the division takes up while fighting at the front line. In order to fit into battle, the division needs to fit into the combat width provided by the battlefield. The less width that each division takes, the more divisions that can fire upon the enemy at the same time. However, making many thin divisions also has its tradeoffs. Different battalion types increase the combat width of a division by a different amount. For example, infantry and armoured battalions use 2 width, artillery battalions use 3 width, and anti-tank and anti-air battalions use 1 width. Support companies do not increase the combat width of a division (see Divisions for details). The Mass Assault land doctrine tree has options that reduce the combat width of infantry battalions, while the mountaineer special forces doctrine tree with Arms Against Tyranny has an option that reduces the combat width of mountaineer battalions. The combat width for a division is the sum of the combat width of all Battalions. Combat width notes:
 - The normal maximum combat width provided by the battlefield varies by terrain.
 - If attacking divisions come from more than one province, then extra width is added to the battlefield for each province, based on the type of terrain that is being attacked.
 - Certain combat tactics can increase or decrease a battle's combat width.
 - It is best to use divisions whose combat widths are factors of the combat width of the terrain you intend to fight in, as this allows you to use as many battalions as possible without sustaining a combat width penalty.
 - Only full divisions can join a fight.
 - Fielding more divisions than the battlefield combat width supports will result in penalties. When this happens, the Battle Interface will automatically put these extra divisions into reserve.
 - Frontage Reduction can reduce the amount of width taken up by divisions, thus allowing more to be used or slightly bigger divisions to be fielded.
- Hardness. Represents what proportion of the division is made up of battalions which are hardened and protected, that is, how "hard" the division is. It is the proportion of hard attacks to soft attacks that an incoming unit receives. Units with more hardness will take less soft attack and more hard attack, while units with less hardness will take more soft attack and less hard attack. Different battalions have different levels of hardness. For example, infantry are very soft (0%), motorized are slightly harder (20%), mechanized are fairly hard (60-75%), medium tank are quite hard (90%), and super heavy tank are the hardest (100%). Multiply this with the attacker's hard attack and multiply 1 - Hardness (i.e. softness) with the attacker's soft attack to find out how many attacks are made against the division. Divisions with high hardness will suffer more of the hard attacks and fewer of the soft attacks directed at them. The hardness for the division is the average of those of all line battalions, but not support companies.

## Other Stats
- Fuel capacity: How much Fuel a unit can store. Higher fuel capacity means that the unit will be able to operate at full capacity longer when it lacks fuel supply. Most units that consume fuel will add FUEL_CAPACITY_DEFAULT_HOURS hours' worth of their fuel usage to the division's fuel capacity, though there are ways to increase this. The stat for the division is the sum of those of all battalions and companies.
- Fuel Usage: How much fuel a unit uses while it is operating. Land units with fuel consumption will consume fuel when moving, in combat, or training. Divisions low on fuel can only be refueled in provinces with enough supply. Using logistics support companies can lower a division's fuel usage. The stat for the division is the sum of those of all battalions and companies, though some support companies do not increase fuel usage despite requiring equipment that normally requires fuel.
- Paradrop: A simple true or false check if the division can be paradropped. For it to be true, all line battalions must be Paratroopers . However, most support companies can be mixed in freely.
- Special Forces Cap: The amount of special forces battalions that a country can field is capped based on the total amount of non-special forces battalions it has deployed. Divisions cannot be changed, trained, given a different template or have their template adjusted if that would result in exceeding that cap. See Special forces.

## Equipment cost
Each division will require a certain amount of manpower , equipment, and training time to produce. Most of these values are simply a sum, except training time, which is based on the longest training time of any Battalion or Company. Reference Training and Exercise.

## Terrain modifiers
(Main article: Terrain)
Terrain can influence the performance of different unit types in offense, defence and even movement. Terrain modifiers are averaged between all Battalions, but those of Support Companies are additive.

# Unit types [verified for 1.9]

NOTE: The table below is not fully accurate. The table below is prepared using first generation equipment. Detailed information for subsequent generations will be found in Land units by unit and for a comparison of equipment of equivalent generation: Land units by year. Generally non-combat statistics are defined per battalion. But combat statistics are a function of equipment and thus not static. Additionally, battalions add modifiers to its equipment. For example, a Marine combat battalion adds +30% Breakthrough to its infantry equipment while an artillery support company has a -40% Soft Attack, -40% Hard Attack, -40% Defence, and -40% Breakthrough to its artillery equipment.
If a battalion uses multiple types of equipment, the stats are a sum of all the equipment used. For example, Mechanized battalions use both Weapon and Mechanized equipment (with a 10% bonus to Weapon Soft Attack and 500% bonus to Weapon Hard Attack). Support Equipment adds nothing to combat stats.

Most land unit battalions can be placed in brigade columns and are often referred to as line battalions. Battalions that need to be placed in the support column are instead called support companies. See division designer for more details.

## Support Companies

Support companies normally do not have combat width, do not affect the speed of divisions, and can be paradropped unless otherwise noted. The equipment stats given here for support companies with armored units are for without No Step Back , where armored vehicles have predetermined stats like other units and can also be made into variants using army exp . With No Step Back , armored vehicles instead receive their stats through the use of the tank designer instead.

# Railway Artillery

 No Step Back
Railway artillery, sometimes referred to as railway guns, are different from other units in a lot of ways.

Production of railway artillery is similar to the production of ships in which the equipment and the unit are essentially one and the same. When one railway artillery piece is produced, it automatically gets deployed in the nation's capital.

Railway artillery movement on land is limited to only on railway lines. Railway artillery can be transported over water by first manually moving it to a railway-connected port before embarking it to another port. Embarked railway artillery ignores naval access level settings, so care must be taken to move them in ways where they avoid sea zones that the player wishes them to avoid.

Railway artillery cannot be assigned to any commanders directly, though like air wings, they can be assigned to land units and will follow their assigned units as best as they can. They can also be assigned to control groups, just like other land units.

Railway artillery doesn't attack other units directly, it instead applies a debuff to nearby enemy units engaged in combat, similar to shore bombardment. If a railway artillery unit has been recently moved, its bombardment bonus will decrease for 24 hours and take some time to recover. If multiple railway artillery are in range of a single battle, only the one with the greatest bombardment will take part.

Railway artillery are affected by supply, including both their movement speed (which decreases to up to -80% in low supply) and their bombardment. Unlike other land units, supply for railway artillery cannot be motorized at a unit- or army-level, so supply hubs must be motorized manually.

Railway artillery can be damaged by enemy Logistics Strike and Strategic Bombing air missions that target railways . 100% of damage from Logistics Strike missions spills over onto railway artillery in the state, while 20% of damage from Strategic Bombing missions spills over onto railway artillery in the state. Damaged railway guns must be repaired with civilian factories and will appear in a country's construction queue.

Railway artillery can be captured when it is overrun or when its controlling country is capitulates or is annexed. When they are overrun, railway artillery have a 20% chance of being captured. This can be increased with higher Equipment Capture Ratio on the overrunning unit. When their controlling country capitulates or is annexed, 50% of their railway artillery pieces are transferred to their new owners.

# Equipment
(Main article: Equipment)
Equipment is produced by Production Lines. All units use Equipment of some sort. An Equipment Type is a more abstract grouping, such as Medium Tanks. A Division that has a Division template that uses Medium Tanks can use any type of Medium Tank Equipment to fill its Equipment needs. The statistics of a unit are determined by the statistics of its equipment types. This may be modified by the unit itself; for example, support units typically get penalties to most combat statistics.
