Infantry technology unlocks new infantry equipment, motorized equipment, and mechanized equipment, as well as improving infantry and cavalry performance as a whole. It also hosts technologies that enable and improve special forces infantry, as well as an additional set of technologies enhancing special forces without Arms Against Tyranny .

Some of these technologies, mainly the ones affecting motorized and mechanized equipment, are interlinked with special projects, providing breakthrough progress in land warfare projects and benefitting from any scientist engaged in passive research in that category. Such technologies have a green ribbons in the lower right-hand corner of their icons.

# Weapons & Equipment

## Infantry weapons [verified for 1.16]

Back to Infantry research tree
Representing an infantryman's standard kit of tools and weapons, Infantry Equipment is the basis of any army. The Basic Infantry equipment technology starts researched for all countries, while all major powers and many minors have the Infantry Equipment I technology researched. As infantry equipment serves as the primary, potentially only, source of stats for most unit types, unlocking and fielding upgraded versions of them earlier than the enemy can provide a significant advantage.

Later technologies also provide infantry with decent hard attack and a small amount of piercing. Although support anti-air and/or support anti-tank companies, even with older equipment, will still provide infantry divisions with far more piercing, especially because 40% of a division's piercing comes from the brigade or support company with the highest amount, the additional hard attack can still make these divisions far more effective against harder opponents, like tanks or mechanized infantry.

## Support weapons [verified for 1.16]

Back to Infantry research tree
Support weapons includes crew-served weapons such as medium and heavy machine guns and mortars. These technologies provide bonuses to the defense and breakthrough to all types of infantry and to cavalry. This reduces incoming damage taken in combat against enemies who have greater soft and/or hard attack (dependent on the given units' hardness), though it provides little to no additional benefit against enemies with lower attack values.

## Night vision [verified for 1.16]

Back to Infantry research tree
Night vision equipment allows soldiers to see even in the dark, offsetting some of the -50% penalty that units receive when fighting at night, giving an edge over enemies who do not have such equipment. When combined with the Night Assault Tactics land doctrine, they can potentially remove all night fighting penalties altogether.

# Mobile Infantry [verified for 1.16]

Back to Infantry research tree
Mobile Infantry technology enables Motorized and Mechanized , as well as motorized versions of artillery brigades, motorized support companies, and other, more specialized motorized brigades.

All mobile infantry technologies are linked to land warfare special projects. They provide breakthrough progress in that category when they are being researched, and they benefit from any scientist engaged in passive research in that category.

## Motorized [verified for 1.16]

Motorized versions of line brigades tend to be much faster than their non-motorized counterparts, have slightly more hardness, and be slightly better on the attack thanks to having more breakthrough, but they also tend to be more expensive, be less reliable, have more penalties on rugged terrain, and require fuel.

In addition to units, motorized technology enables and can enhance motorized logistics. Motorized supply extends the range of supply hub and naval base , allowing them to supply units that are much further away. However, this comes at the cost of motorized equipment, both when enabling motorization and when said motorized equipment suffers losses from breakdowns in rugged terrain and/or bad weather and/or losses from enemy logistics strikes.

## Mechanized [verified for 1.16]

Mechanized technologies focus on improving Mechanized units and can benefit from motorized technology bonuses. Compared to Motorized , Mechanized are slower, more expensive, and more fuel-intensive (though they are still much faster than ordinary Infantry ), but they have far greater hardness and provide a lot more defense and HP. Their production costs can also be potentially through designing a variant using army exp , which, when coupled with how few equipment losses they suffer thanks to their high HP, can actually make them more affordable in practice than Motorized once the combat casualties start rolling in. Although their offensive stats lack compared to tanks', with enough upgrades, Mechanized can also be surprisingly capable on the offense and can even go toe-to-toe with enemy tanks when used alongside Motorized Anti-Tank units.

FOCUS Mechanized Equipment I: Mechanized infantry is infantry equipped with armored personnel carriers (APCs) or infantry fighting vehicles (IFVs) for transport and combat. Mechanized infantry is distinguished from motorized infantry in that its vehicles provide a degree of protection from hostile fire, as opposed to soft-skinned wheeled vehicles (trucks or jeeps) for motorized infantry.

## Amphibious Tractors [verified for 1.16]

 Amtrac or Amtracs are special forces versions of Mechanized more suited to wet terrain and naval invasions, and as special forces, are subject to a country's special forces cap. Although they have less breakthrough than their mechanized counterparts, consume a lot more fuel, and cannot be customized using army exp to discount them in the same way, amtracs not only receive terrain bonuses in Marshes , Rivers , and Amphibious landings, but they also benefit from special forces attack bonuses from, e.g., special forces doctrines and Commando military high command.

These technologies can also benefit from both motorized and mechanized technology bonuses, as well as technology bonuses for naval transports.

## Armored Cars [verified for 1.16]

 La Résistance
 Armored Car and their armored car recon counterparts are avaialble with La Résistance . These are effectively weaker, softer, much faster, and slightly cheaper alternatives to Light Tank and Armored Recon , respectively. In theory, their niche is would be for somewhat of a hybrid role between light tanks and motorized infantry, though players may struggle to find a practical use for this niche.

With No Step Back , they unfortunately suffer from being much less customizable than their tank-based counterparts. Thanks to the tank designer, once their Light Tank counterparts have become obsolete later in the game, they can get up-gunned and/or repurposed into light td , light sp art , or light spaa , whereas obselete armored car upgrades are limited to incremental variants where they are stuck with their outdated equipment forever. With Arms Against Tyranny , they further suffer from requiring their own, dedicated military industrial organizations that cannot be used for any other type of equipment, whereas their tank-based counterparts share their military industrial organizations with those that can also be used for other types of tanks, thereby offering industrial synergies that armored cars lack.

These technologies can also benefit from motorized and armor technology bonuses.

# Special forces [verified for 1.16]

Back to Infantry research tree
Special forces doctrine

In addition to regular, motorized, and mechanized infantry, there are three specialized infantry types, known as Special Forces or SF (not to be confused with the land doctrine Superior Firepower): Marines , Mountaineers , and Paratroopers . These units are useful in specific situations. They only require standard infantry equipment, but tend to require more of it compared to Infantry .

 amtrac and amphibious tank units also count as special forces units. The former are closer to Mechanized units than to Infantry and require their own, specialized motorized equipment. The latter are armored units that rely on armor technology. With No Step Back , they can also be built out of light amphibious tank , medium amphibious tank , and heavy amphibious tank chasses using the tank designer.

Each country has a special forces cap that dictates the maximum amount of special forces battalions they are allowed to field. This cap is the greater of either 24 battalions or 5% of the total amount of non-SF battalions making up the rest of your deployed divisions. Divisions cannot be changed, trained, given a different template or have their template adjusted if that would result in exceeding the SF cap. By default, to increase the SF cap beyond the default 24 without any bonuses, you would have to exceed the 480 non-SF battalion breakeven point by 20 for each additional SF battalion. For example, you would need 500 non-SF battalions for 25 SF battalions, 520 non-SF battalions for 26 SF battalions, 540 non-SF battalions for 27 SF battalions, etc. These battalions do not need to be fully equipped or be at full strength, they just need to be part of divisions that are deployed in the field.

Special forces cap can also be increased through certain political advisors, military high command posts, national focuses, and decisions that affect national spirits. With Arms Against Tyranny , the amount of special forces that can be fielded can be increased through special forces doctrines, both by increasing special forces cap and by decreasing the contribution of certain units to that cap. Without Arms Against Tyranny , special forces cap can be increased with the Expanded Special Forces Program Special Forces Concentration technology.

## Marines [verified for 1.16]

 Marines are specialized infantry trained to engage in wet terrain and perform amphibious invasions. They receive bonuses when attacking over wet terrain and during naval invasions. Marine units are very effective when supported by Engineer or their Pioneers , Armored Engineer , or Assault Engineer cousins.

## Paratroopers [verified for 1.16]

 Paratroopers are the only type of unit that can be paradropped into the field, which they do via transport plane. This allows them to perform operations behind enemy lines and in locations where other units cannot easily reach. It is important to note that divisions that mix Paratroopers with other line battalions cannot be paradropped.

The AI will use paratroopers in battle, but never uses them for paradrops.

## Special Forces Concentration [verified for 1.16]

 Arms Against Tyranny Arms Against Tyranny doctrines Special forces doctrine

These technologies increase the capabilities, and potentially also the number, of special forces units. The first tech will give them a boost to acclimatization speed. Afterwards, the tree splits into two options. One option is to train your special forces harder, to improve their skills and their ability to fight for longer before having to be resupplied. The other option is to expand the special forces training programs to accept more recruits: your special forces will be more numerous, but come with more drag and not quite as high speed. In the end, though, they will still be elite forces and will be able to develop training to make them even more skilled in fighting in the harshest of conditions.
