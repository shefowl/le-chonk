Air doctrine allows a nation to specialize the way its air force conducts air warfare. Air doctrines can be reviewed and unlocked from the Officer Corps using air exp . There are three mutually exclusive doctrine trees.
3

# Doctrine Overviews [verified for 1.16]

- Strategic Destruction
- By bombing the enemies factories, either by night, or more dangerously, during the day we can seriously hinder their war machine.

This is a doctrine focused primarily on offensive strategic bombing and the fighters that are meant to escort these bombers. It provides a plethora of bonuses to fighters early on in its tree, then fills the latter half of the tree with various bonuses to strategic bombing. It also provides the best bonuses to naval bombers, including those launched from carriers. Its exclusive Officer Corps spirits of Industrial Destruction and Total Devastation encourage the use of strategic bombers. However, it provides next to no bonuses to close air support besides reducing their disruption from enemy fighters. It also forces the country to choose whether to receive bonuses to interception or air superiority for fighters, preventing them from being used more flexibly.

Historically, strategic bombing was deployed extensively by all sides during the second world war. However, it was primarily the air forces of the United Kingdom and the United States that would field large fleets of heavy bombers meant to destroy enemy industry, infrastructure, and morale. Allied strategic bombing campaigns in Europe and Asia had a famously pivotal role in their victory over the Axis. The German Reich, having had their heavy bomber program atrophy in favor of dive bombers, also tried their hand at strategic bombing later in the war with their V1, V2, and V3 Revenge Weapon programs to much more limited success.

Major air powers who wish to use strategic bombing to destroy their enemies' infrastructure and industry can benefit a lot from following this doctrine. However, because all of its bonuses to fighters are all available early in its tree, even more minor air powers with limited access to air exp can find this doctrine useful.
- Battlefield Support
- By improving how close air support interacts with divisions we can lay waste to enemy divisions with optimal support.

This doctrine is overwhelmingly focused on close air support and their ability to support ground troops in land battles. While it gives some bonuses to fighters, naval bombers, and strategic bombing, the bulk of its bonuses are geared towards close air support, air support, and the ground support received by ground troops as a result of air support. Its exclusive Officer Corps spirits of Dive Bombing and Continuous Strike not only encourage the use of close air support aircraft, but can also make them surprisingly good at shooting back at any intercepting fighters when they are equipped with the Air Attack to do so. However, this focus on close air support comes at the cost of almost everything else. This doctrine has the worst bonuses to fighters out of all three doctrines and provides very few bonuses to large airframes performing strategic bombing. Even though tactical bombers do nominally benefit from both ground support bonuses and several strategic bombing bonuses, they are much more vulnerable with this doctrine thanks to how much worse the fighters that would need to escort them would perform against interceptors.

Dive bombing would see a lot of attention and development in the run-up to the second world war, especially by the forces of the United Kingdom, the United Kingdom, Japan, and the German Reich. Although the first three would primarily focus their dive bomber efforts on naval torpedo bombers, the German Reich would whole-heartedly embrace their use for supporting ground troops. This would be mirrored by the Soviet Union, who made extensive use of small, single-engine bombers in their fight against their foes.

As advertized, this doctrine is very useful for air powers who plan on primarily using close air support and little to nothing else in their air forces. However, this doctrine is also the most useful one for minor powers without an air force, as their ground forces can still benefit from the ground support bonuses given by this doctrine when they are supported by allied air forces.
- Operational Integrity
- Tactical bombers are flexible and can perform both ground support and regular bombing.

This is a somewhat generalist doctrine that focuses a lot on flexible use of fighters and avoiding losses for bombers. It does also improve naval bombing as well as strategic bombing done by smaller, tactical bombers, but the bulk of its bonuses are given to fighters, both on the offense when trying obtain air superiority and on the defense when trying to intercept enemy bombers. When fully complete, the fighter bonuses from this doctrine are better than any other doctrine (though the difference between it and Strategic Destruction are quite small). Its exclusive Officer Corps spirits of Material Destruction and Massed Strike benefit the use of tactical bombers and fighters for establishing air superiority. Finally, this is doctrine offers the highest amount of air wing experience gain out of all doctrines, which means air wings that survive air missions improve in effectiveness faster, and air wings that take losses lose effectiveness slower. The doctrine definitely has its drawbacks, though. It provides very few bonuses to close air support or strategic bombers, and although it can provide the best stats for fighters to help establish air superiority, once that has been established, it also provides worse air superiority bonuses compared to both other doctrines and so often needs more fighters to get the same benefits, even with the help of Massed Strike . Unlike Strategic Destruction, its fighter bonuses are also scattered throughout their tree, requiring a lot more air exp investment to unlock comparable fighter bonuses.

Although tactical and strategic levels of military thinking have a long history, the idea of there needing to be dedicated considerations for an in-between, so-called "operational" level of thinking was very new at the time of the second world war. This extended to the use of air forces as well and their ability to be used not just tactically against ground combat forces or strategically against enemy industry and infrastructure, but also operationally against operational targets like supply depots, combat airfields, and signaling infrastructure. While German Reich's and Japan's air forces would find success targeting their enemies' air fields in the opening moves of some of their major operations, it was eventually the Allied forces of the United Kingdom, the United States, and the Soviet Union that would put this practice to its best use.

Having a more generalist focus, this doctrine is well-suited for air powers who are not sure how they will want to use their air forces going forward, as well as by those who know they will focus heavily on fighters. While focusing on fighters can be cost-effective for minor powers who expect to have to face much larger and better-equipped air forces themselves, the high air exp requirements of this doctrine can prove to be an obstacle.

# Researching Doctrines [verified for 1.16]

Each individual doctrine has a base cost of 100 air exp to unlock. This cost may be modified by spirits, advisors, and special bonuses from focuses, events, or decisions.

It is possible to change doctrine tree at any point, but all bonuses from previously unlocked doctrines are lost when unlocking a different doctrine tree. Any doctrine-exclusive Officer Corps spirits are also automatically switched to different ones when a different doctrine tree is selected.

## Starting Doctrines

Some nations start the game with a air doctrine pre-selected and unlocked. They can still change doctrines as normal, though sticking with their starting doctrine can cost them less air exp in the long run.

# Definitions and Terminology [verified for 1.16]

- Air Superiority Mission Efficiency: Improves air attack, air defense, and agility for planes on this mission. See Missions.
- Air Superiority: Increases the contribution of planes on Air Superiority to their air zone's air superiority. See Air superiority.
- Escort Efficiency: Reduces disruption from enemy attacks.
- Interception Mission Efficiency: Improves air attack, air defense, and agility for planes on this mission. See Missions.
- Air Support Mission Efficiency: Improves air attack, air defense, and agility for planes on this mission. See Missions.
- Naval Mission Efficiency: Improves air attack, air defense, and agility for planes on this mission. See Missions.
- Bomber Defense: Increases the air defense for planes on Strategic Bombing mission. See Air Defense.
- Strategic Bombing: Improves Strategic Bombing damage for planes on that mission.
- Ace generation chance: Increase the probability of generating aces during missions.
- Night-time strategic bombing penalty: Reduces the negative effects of night for planes on Strategic Bombing mission, notably the Ground Bombing Targeting penalty.
- Strategic Bombing Visibility: Counteracts enemy detection when on Strategic Bombing mission, reducing the maximum number of enemy planes responding to the bombers. See Detection, Disruption, Damage.
- Fighter Detection: Increases the amount of detection planes on Air Superiority mission contribute to the region. See Detection, Disruption, Damage.
- Interception Detection: Increases the amount of detection planes on Interception mission contribute to the region. See Detection, Disruption, Damage.
- Ground Support : Increases the scaling rate of the Enemy Air Support bonus provided by air superiority. See Air superiority.
- Enemy Air Support: Modifies the negative effect enemy air superiority has on the combat ability of our ground troops. See Land_battle#Combat_factors.
