The Soviet Union (officially “Union of Soviet Socialist Republics” or “USSR”) is a major country and the leader of the Comintern faction. It is rich in natural resources and has decent technology and an exponentially growing industry, alongside a population of 161.6 M. It controls almost the entire northern third of Asia as well as the eastern edges of Europe.

In many ways, the USSR is the epicenter of Communist ideology, which finds itself at odds with many of their neighbors. With various territorial disputes with the Non-Aligned Finland, Baltic States and Poland to the west, and frequent border clashes with the Fascist Japan to the East (not to mention their Fascist allies Germany and Italy to the further West/Southwest of Poland becoming more and more ambitious in their aims), it seems all but likely that a war will start in one way or another, and only through extensive preparations can the worst be avoided, and perhaps pushback and triumph over their enemies.

Despite their large amount of manpower and natural resources, the USSR (in 1936) starts off suffering from a variety of drawbacks.

Their country's stability is torn apart as a result of old power struggles and paranoia, with purges on a regular basis intended to root out all conspirators with often detrimental side effects to the efficiency of management, production and command. Their military is in need of rapid modernization in multiple aspects to keep up with their European neighbors. And the Five Year Plan has only just started recently and would still require effort and time to reap the fruits.

With time, effort, and a wise management many of these obstacles can be overcome or redirected, and USSR could potentially become a grand superpower similar to the real-life equivalent.

# Historical background

The Russian Empire’s collapse in the chaos of the 1917 revolutions and the ensuing civil war gave rise to the Union of Soviet Socialist Republics in 1922. Born from the Bolshevik victory over the White forces and foreign intervention, the new Soviet state emerged under the leadership of Vladimir Lenin and the Communist Party, committed to building a socialist society and spreading revolutionary ideology abroad. The early years were marked by economic hardship, reconstruction, and the suppression of political dissent.

Following Lenin’s death in 1924, Joseph Stalin gradually consolidated power, outmaneuvering rivals within the party. By the early 1930s, the Soviet Union had embarked on a program of rapid industrialization and forced collectivization of agriculture, transforming the nation’s economic base at the cost of widespread famine, suffering, and political repression. The Great Purge of 1936–1938 further tightened Stalin’s grip, eliminating perceived enemies within the military, party, and intelligentsia, but leaving the Red Army weakened on the eve of global conflict.

In foreign affairs, the Soviet Union initially sought collective security against the rise of fascism, engaging in negotiations with the United Kingdom and France. However, Western reluctance to commit to firm guarantees led Moscow to sign the Molotov-Ribbentrop Pact with the German Reich in August 1939, a non-aggression treaty that secretly divided Eastern Europe into spheres of influence. Following the pact, the USSR annexed eastern Poland, the Baltic states, and Bessarabia, and fought the Winter War against Finland, gaining territory but revealing serious deficiencies in its armed forces.

This uneasy arrangement with Germany ended abruptly on 22 June 1941, when the Wehrmacht launched Operation Barbarossa, the largest invasion in history. The Soviet Union, caught off guard, suffered devastating early losses but gradually halted the German advance through immense sacrifice and the mobilization of its vast resources. The turning points at Moscow, Stalingrad, and Kursk shifted the momentum, and by 1945 the Red Army had pushed deep into Eastern Europe, capturing Berlin in May. Victorious yet scarred by immense human and material losses, the Soviet Union emerged from the war as a superpower, dominating Eastern Europe and entering into a global rivalry with the United States that would define the Cold War.

# Geography [verified for 1.17]

The Soviet Union is a massive country, stretching from the barren wastelands of Kamchatka to the warm, fertile fields of Ukraine. Much of the north and east is forest, with polar regions of Siberia marshlands and plains. There is little mountains or hills besides the Caucasus and Ural ranges. The south is primarily plains, with a few urban provinces dispersed throughout the country.

## Diplomacy
As the premier communist nation in 1936, the Soviet Union is the head of the Comintern, consisting of Tannu Tuva and Mongolia. Meanwhile, Communist China, and Sinkiang are the only other countries that have a favorable view of the USSR.

Should Republican Spain prevail in the Spanish Civil War, it may be amenable to joining, as would a communist-aligned France.

# Strategies and guides

## Stalin's Great Purges
This is a guide about Stalins great purges, which happens when the appropriate focuses (The Zinovyevite Terrorist Center, The Anti-Soviet Trotskyist Center, The Military Conspiracy, The Bloc of Rights and Trotskyites) finish (and it assumes you follow the centre path, some of the other paths might change who gets purged). After each one you get an unavoidable purge of several characters. The characters purged are always the same, unless a random purge already purged one of those characters earlier via the paranoia system, or if they are exiled. If the character was already purged, a random character will be picked. So the amount of people purged cannot be reduced.

- The first purge kills: Grigory Zinovyev, Lev Kamenev, Ivan Smirnov, Grigory Sokolnikov, Aleksandr Shlyapnikov
- The second purge kills: Karl Radek, Ivar Smilga, Yevgeny Preobrazhensky, Martemyan Ryutin, and if you own No Step Back, Nikolay Ustryalov
- The third purge, the military purge kills: Mikhail Tukhachevsky, Aleksandr Yegorov, Vasily Blyukher, Vitaly Primakov
- The last purge kills: Nikolay Bukharin, Aleksey Rykov, Mikhail Tomsky, Maksim Litvinov
After a great purge event fires, a random purge event cannot happen for 15 days.

## Overthrowing Stalin and putting Bukharin in charge
With No Step Back released, one notable option available to players who stay communist is the ability to get rid of Stalin without a civil war if they go down the Right Opposition Path. However, this is very risky, since you need to assassinate Stalin before Beria becomes head of the NKVD, and should Paranoia get above 90, the civil war will fire. But if you want Russia to avoid another civil war, read on.

First, one should decide one thing: if they want to be cooperative with the Left Opposition, or if they wish to save the generals Stalin historically purges. This will determine focus order. Regardless, the first 3 focuses one should take are The Path of Marx-Leninism, Right Opposition, and The Need for Policy Changes - for the political power you get, either hire Mikhail Kalinin or take Improved Worker Conditions for a much needed stability boost (note that until Bukharin is in charge, you will not be able to earn much political power - although you can somewhat offset this by infiltrating the civilian governments of other countries; if playing on ahistorical, there's a chance Turkey might back down when you mobilize the armed forces in response to them remilitarizing the Bosporus, thus giving you some stability and political power). After that, take Infiltrate the NKVD and Gain Support from Party Members, then go up to Partial Mobilization, and round it off with Divert Attention Towards the Military - while you might think that Eliminate the Left is a good idea if not working with the Left Opposition, doing so will boost Paranoia, so it's best avoided. However, if you want to attack another country, consider holding off to see if you can go for War Economy instead of Partial Mobilization after justifying.

While this may seem like you will not finish the latter before the Zinovyevite Terror Center finishes, the game will prioritize the player's focus as long as it completes on the same day (and both of the latter focuses will bypass the Terror Center). Additionally, if we take the focus that bypasses the aforementioned Center focus before, Stalin will merely speed up his focuses, whereas if we let it play out but not finish, he'll be on the same track but we'll be closer. While it might seem ideal to purge army officers because of how many you have, if you need to lower paranoia, focus on purging the navy - while you have more generals, the navy is not as important to the Soviet Union as it is to the British or Japanese. Generate new admirals as needed, and also forge production reports if you don't have the option to purge an admiral. Only purge officers if you have no other choice.

It should be noted that while Beria will be the one who can lock off the bloodless coup, if Yagoda is purged, he will be replaced with Nikolay Yezhov - this is both good and bad, since while doing so will significantly lower paranoia, Yezhov will generate more paranoia on a weekly basis than Yagoda does (and he also lowers Stability more). The best case scenario would be Yagoda getting purged late (if he gets purged at all), but as long as you can attempt to eliminate Stalin before Yezhov is purged, you're in the clear. Note that you can decline to purge Yezhov, but doing so will boost paranoia and cost you some political power.

Anyways, what happens next also depends on who you want to save.
- If you are going to save the generals who get purged, rush towards Plan for the Coup.
- If you are planning on saving the advisors, take Cooperation against Stalin, and either Align the Zinovyevites or Liberate Ryuten - you hypothetically have time for both, but the more time you waste, the more chance you risk of Stalin purging advisors who you otherwise would save. Afterwards, rush Plan for the Coup.

Regardless, as soon as Plan for the Coup is finished, immediately click the decision to sway the head of the NKVD to your cause, and take Dissent in the Party. It should finish before the NKVD head is swayed to your cause, so go straight for Coup 'etat, and clicking the decision to eliminate Stalin. You have an 80% chance of Stalin dying this way - consider disbanding the entire army beforehand if he survives, since you'll likely get a civil war afterwards. However, if he does die, congrats! But you're not out of the woods just yet - Lavrentiy Beria, who will replace Stalin, will continue down the Centre path, and should he finish Secure the Administration, the Bloodless coup option will be locked out, in which case, all your work will have been for naught. Afterwards, all that's left is to take either Return Democracy to the Party or The Enemies of the State to remove the Trotskyite Plot debuff.

## First Strike

One relatively easy way to deal with the Germany problem as the Soviet Union is to strike early and to strike hard. You start with nearly triple the manpower of Germany, though you have various debuffs and equipment shortages. Use your first political power to justify on Poland. Build military factories, run exercises to generate Army Experience, hire Chief of Army, switch to War Economy when your war support is over 50%. Update your infantry template to 9 infantry / 1 artillery + support artillery, engineers, recon, logistics. (You'll need to produce lots of artillery and supply equipment.) Disband units as necessary (in particularly, get rid of all NKVD Pogranichnaya Diviziya ASAP) to put 80-ish fully equipped infantry divisions on the Polish border. Likewise, disband some of your mechanized divisions. Declare on Poland, then start justifying on Germany. If all goes well, you'll capitulate Germany by early 1938, earning you "We don't really like statistics", "The Revolution Triumphant" (if you puppet some part of Germany instead of annexing it), a good shot at "Not a Step Back". (NOTE: you won't get "One Step Forward" unless you declare on Germany while you're still fighting Poland, which is risky.) Everyone will hate you (you'll generate 100% world tension all by yourself), and the UK will guarantee any Democratic country you attempt to justify on after Germany, but you'll be out of immediate danger. You can immediately follow by justifying on Austria (which, being unaligned/fascist, is fair game, as far as the UK is concerned), and then either justifying or using the "Eradicate Western Fascism" focus to attack Italy for "The Pope? How many Divisions does he have?". (Don't dawdle: with Germany gone and the Axis dismantled, the Allies may decide to invade and take Rome as early as 1940.)

## Japan War

It's possible to drag Soviet Union into Sino-Japanese war, by guaranteeing Communist China and Sinkiang. As such, when Japan invades China, Soviet Union ends up dragged in it. Don't forget to move divisions to Japan front beforehand in this case. Since you have more divisions (both if directly comparing USSR and Japan - and if factoring in puppets, China, and Volunteers from German Reich and Italy), and other Axis factions don't participate in this - defeating Japan should be rather easy. The only tricky part may be invasion of Japan mainland - as USSR's navy is small, and starts on opposite side of the globe.

That can allow to remove debuffs sooner, protect Communist China, and defeat Japan if played correctly. Japan, Manchukuo, Mengkukuo, Korea, Federated States of Micronesia, Mariana Federation can be turned into Puppets - don't forget to take their industry and resources - though, China countries would also try to get parts of Japan during peace conference. You must also consider the time needed to move divisions to China and back - in case you're planning to bum-rush elsewhere, or otherwise go to war with someone.
