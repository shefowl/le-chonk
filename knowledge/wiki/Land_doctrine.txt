Land doctrine allows a nation to specialize the way its army conducts land warfare. Land doctrines are split into Grand Doctrines and Subdoctrines. They can be reviewed and unlocked from the Officer Corps using army exp . There are four mutually exclusive grand doctrines and have unique milestones for each track when its unlocked subdoctrine is mastered. 3

# Doctrine Tracks [verified for 1.17]

Tracks are split into four categories: Infantry, Artillery & Combat Support, Armor and Operations. Each has a set of subdoctrines shared among each grand doctrine. Mastery is used to unlock milestones within the subdoctrine, granting further bonuses. Each track gains mastery when certain units engage in action, or at a reduced rate while training. If a subdoctrine is not selected, up to 200 Mastery is held in reserve.

Doctrine Track: Infantry
Gains Mastery from:: all infantry

Doctrine Track: Artillery & Combat Support
Gains Mastery from:: artillery , support artillery , support

Doctrine Track: Armor
Gains Mastery from:: armored car , all tanks, all armor

Doctrine Track: Operations
Gains Mastery from:: all infantry, artillery , support artillery , all tanks, all armor

When a track as been mastered, it unlocks the grand doctrine milestone for the track. One of the milestones increases Brigade size and each milestone (excluding Grand Battleplan Operations) enables a Tactic.

# Doctrine Overviews [verified for 1.16]

- Mobile Warfare
- Hit them where they don't expect - mobile warfare doctrine seeks to break the trench deadlock of the First World War via aggressive maneuvers to pre-empt and disrupt the enemy.

This doctrine is concerned with the employment of motorized, mechanized, and armored units, representing a doctrine of fast movement and extended attacks. The bulk of its bonuses are to division organization, movement speed, and breakthrough, as well as to supporting modifiers like organization loss from movement and planning speed. What it lacks in stats compared to Superior Firepower and Grand Battleplan, this doctrine is meant to make up for in organization and speed. Its exclusive Officer Corps spirits of Embrace the Future , Motorization Drive , and Maneuver Warfare similarly emphasize these traits and the use of motorized, mechanized, and armored units. Additionally, its most important tank division bonuses become available far earlier than any other land doctrine, giving them an edge in earlier wars with tanks. However, compared to other land doctrines, this doctrine lacks any real combat stat bonuses to its units (besides breakthrough for tank battalions), relying more on their higher organization to make up for the lower stats. This can make equipment and manpower losses under this doctrine far worse compared to other doctrines, as the higher organization cannot offset receiving more HP damage. Even if one wishes to use the additional organization to switch out an organization-providing unit like infantry for a harder-hitting unit like tanks, those higher-organization units tend to also be the ones with more HP, so such a setup would also lower the HP of the division and still result in more manpower and equipment losses. Also, while the doctrine gives a lot of breakthrough to tanks (though far less to other types of armored units), with No Step Back 's tank designer, well-designed tanks tend to already have more than enough breakthrough, making the additional benefits from the doctrine less useful in practice.
The first set of mutually exclusive choices is between Mobile Infantry (left 1) or Blitzkrieg (right 1), while the second is between Desperate Defense (left 2) and Modern Blitzkrieg (right 2). Mobile Infantry adds a lot more organization and general bonuses to motorized and mechanized units, including those found in armored divisions, while Blitzkrieg adds a little more organization and a lot of breakthrough to tank units (but not tank variants like tank destroyers) as well as enables the powerful offensive Breakthrough tactic to punch through combat quickly. Desperate Defense expands the country's recruitable population and unlocks the highly effective defensive Guerrilla Tactics that can stall out enemies, while Modern Blitzkrieg continues adding more organization and breakthrough to land units and unlocks the potent defensive Backhand Blow tactic that can counter enemy attempts at armored breakthroughs.

Historically, Auftragstaktik, or "mission-style tactics", was a doctrine that the German Empire experimented with in the run-up to the first world war. Rather than the old style of commanding an army by issuing orders from the top, mission-style tactics dictated that armies should be commanded by giving out objectives and goals and allowing lower-ranked officers to come up with their own way of achieving them. Fully developed for infantry over the course of the war, it would be applied in the interwar years to tanks and dedicated armored divisions. The German Reich would go on to demonstrate the effectiveness of this combination in the early stages of the second world war, making Allied observers give it the name "blitzkrieg", or "lightning warfare" (though it was not referred to as such by the Germans). The efficacy shown by mission-style tactics throughout the war would end up cementing it as a key part of most post-war military doctrines.

As advertised, this doctrine is best suited for countries that wish to make a lot of use of fast, mobile units to rapidly exploit breakthroughs in the enemy line before it can be filled by reserves. Nations with limited industrial and/or manpower capacities should be wary of the increased losses they should expect to suffer under this doctrine thanks to it increasing organization.
- Superior Firepower
- Send shells, not men - Superior Firepower emphasizes employing crushing masses of fire to pave the way for maneuver units to advance and capture territory.

 This generalist doctrine emphasizes raw stats of the army. While Mobile Warfare gives a lot of organization to armies following it, and Grand Battleplan gives a lot of bonuses through planning and entrenchment to armies following it, this doctrine just gives pure, simple stat bonuses. These bonuses are useful no matter what composition the army ends up having or purpose it ends up needing to serve. The exclusive Officer Corps spirits of this doctrine of Engineering Schools , Overwhelming Firepower , and Smoke and Fire also continue this idea of providing generalist bonuses where there is always something useful no matter the situation. The downside of this generalist approach is that this doctrine is also outshined by other doctrines that have more specialized bonuses that present a better package for specific situations or uses. Many of its more impactful bonuses are also found in the last two options of the doctrine's tree, making it lackluster in situations where army exp is in short supply.
The first set of mutually exclusive choices is between Dispersed Support (left 1) or Integrated Support (right 1), while the second is between AirLand Battle (left 2) and Shock & Awe (right 2). Dispersed Support primarily provides benefits to line artillery brigades (and their variants) and makes them punch harder, while Integrated Support improves the effectiveness of all support companies, especially the ones with high soft attack like support artillery and rangers . AirLand Battle provides additional hard attack, especially for armored units, as well as bonuses to air superiority to allow aircraft to provide more bonuses to land units, while Shock & Awe provides more soft attack to all units, but especially to infantry and artillery, as well as more army organization. Both choices give access to the powerful offensive Breakthrough tactic to punch through combat quickly, as well as the annoying defensive Tactical Withdrawal tactic that can trap enemies in a constant cat-and-mouse game that prevents them from using their own special tactics.

The tremendous losses in manpower from the first world war had a huge impact on a lot of both military and political thought afterward. One of the many reactions to the seminal catastrophe was that every life is precious, and that an army fights better when even its lowliest grunts know that their army will do whatever it can to keep them alive. Of the many nations who fought the second world war, none took this idea to heart more so than the United States, equipping and fielding not only a large amount of field hospitals and battlefield medicine units, but also putting a lot of effort into making the lives of their soldiers easier. This was also seen in how their soldiers were fed, with supply ships dedicated to carrying ice cream in the Pacific theater being a particularly famous example.

As a generalist doctrine, this doctrine can work well for almost any strategy, though it will rarely be the best pick for any one approach. When taken with Integrated Support, it can particularly benefit divisions that make full use of support companies. Minor nations can potentially make great use of this doctrine by sending their forces as expeditionaries to larger powers who have taken Grand Battleplan, as the planning and entrenchment bonuses from it will stack with the stats bonuses of this doctrine. For countries for whom army exp is at a premium, it should be noted that a lot of the important bonuses for this doctrine come later than normal compared to other doctrines, so it is important to start getting army exp early when using this doctrine.
- Grand Battleplan
- With sufficient preparation, no obstacle is insurmountable - Grand Battleplan is characterized by patient, meticulous preparation of all arms, in both attack and defense.

 This doctrine is one that revolves around obtaining strong battlefield performance through planning and entrenchment. While its stat bonuses can be rather lackluster compared to Superior Firepower and its organization is paltry compared to Mobile Warfare, it can beat both doctrines on the defense when it is allowed to fully entrench and both doctrines on the offense when it is allowed to obtain the maximum amount of planning. Unexpectedly, its exclusive Officer Corps spirits of Theatre Training , Tip of the Spear , and Logistical Focus provide generally useful bonuses to their armies instead of emphasizing their need for planning and entrenchnment even further. It also has a hidden strength in providing the most amount of breakthrough to non-armored battalions, which is a combat stat these battalions tend to be sorely missing. Unfortunately, the greatest strengths of this doctrine are also the source of its main weaknesses. On the offense, it can take a while to build up enough planning to reach parity with the combat performance of other doctrines, and on the defense, active combat will degrade entrenchment and the movement of units will outright reset it, making it poor for mobile/elastic defense. In both cases, this doctrine is also particularly vulnerable to spy networks, which can degrade and even outright kill both planning and entrenchment bonuses of enemy divisions. There are also some scenarios, e.g., naval invasions without the Expeditionary Units special forces doctrine or paradrops, where planning is not available, and so this doctrine can underperform in these situations.
The only set of mutually exclusive choices for this doctrine is between Assault (left) and Infiltration (right). Assault provides more offensive benefits through planning, especially to tanks, and give access to the powerful offensive Breakthrough tactic to punch through combat quickly. Infiltration provides more bonuses to infantry, makes up for having less max planning by greatly increasing the combat effectiveness of all units at night, and is generally seen as a more cautious and defensive-oriented path.

War planning has an extensive history, but it was truly taken to the next level during and after the first world war. Operations like Russian Empire's Brusilov Offensive, Italy's Isonzo Campaign, France's Nivelle Offensive, the German Empire's spring 1918 offensives, and the United Kingdom's, France's, and the United States' Hundred Days Offensive ran the whole gamut of disastrous defeat to decisive victory, and all involved extensive planning before their execution. Reliance on war planning would continue on in the second world war with mixed success, including the German Reich's various case plans like Case Blue and Case Anton, France's Saar Offensive and more-successful participation in Operation Overlord and Operation Dragoon, and Japan's and the United States' thoroughly wargamed naval operations in the Pacific. War planning would continue to be of key importance after the war, with the Soviet Union taking a particular liking to meticulously planning out potential operations.

Assuming espionage is not a major concern, this doctrine can be useful as a somewhat generalist doctrine with great offensive potential, especially with tanks, on the Assault path and a more generalist approach that is a bit more defensively-minded on the Infiltration path. The high generic breakthrough bonuses can compound with the extra planning from this doctrine to greatly reduce losses when on the offense with non-tank units (e.g., special forces infantry or mechanized divisions). Many countries also start with the first doctrine in this tree already completed at the start of the game, which can effectively discount the amount of army exp required to complete it.
- Mass Assault
- Our willingness to sacrifice guarantees our victory - Mass Assault focuses on concentrating numerical superiority to force a breakthrough at the chosen time and place.

 This rather odd doctrine is actually two different doctrines masquerading as one. While the mutually exclusive choices of other doctrines merely supplement their doctrine's overall approach, this doctrine's mutually exclusive choice between Deep Battle (left) and Mass Mobilization (right) present two paths that are so radically different that they effectively create two different doctrines under one larger category. Their exclusive Officer Corps spirits of The Queen of Battle , Bayonett Strength , and Operational Reserves seem to emphasize relying on infantry in battle and reducing equipment and manpower losses through more HP, but how they are expected to be used will change depending on what is chosen between Deep Battle and Mass Mobilization.
Deep Battle is a jack-of-all-trades doctrine that is a master of none, even more so than the generalist Superior Firepower. It is most comparable to Grand Battleplan, providing a much weaker version of its bonuses that are supplemented by a few bonuses borrowed from Mobile Warfare, namely organization loss reduction from movement and access to the strong defensive Backhand Blow tactic. It adds to these a few of its own bonuses, of which the most notable are reduced combat width on infantry battalions that effectively acts as a stat bonus for them and a major bonus to reinforce rate, helping to prevent premature enemy breakthroughs so long as there are friendly units remaining across all sections of a frontline. This variety of bonuses is also its major downside, however. While it may seem like a more generalist choice similar to Superior Firepower, in practice, each of these bonuses pulls the doctrine in a different direction in terms of how to best make use of its modifiers. For example, reduced supply consumption, high reinforce rate, and reduced organization loss from movement seem to encourage an approach to defensive infantry that revolves around massing a large amount of smaller divisions that can recover after retreats and reinforce ongoing battles faster than the enemy can deplete all of the defending divisions' organization, but increased max entrenchment and the general lack of organization bonuses to infantry compared to other doctrines seem to discourage this approach over one of large, heavily entrenched divisions relying on engineer and static defense.
Mass Mobilization is sort of a one-trick pony doctrine whose one trick just happens to be incredibly powerful. It gives bonuses almost exclusively to infantry units, but the bonuses it does give make it peerless for helping to fill out and hold any frontline. These include an incredibly high reinforce rate to prevent premature breakthroughs, a very high recovery rate especially on infantry , access to the highly effective defensive Guerrilla Tactics that can stall out enemies, greatly reduced combat width usage of infantry that indirectly functions as a general stat bonus, and extra recruitable population to make manpower much less of a concern. The fact that it requires less army exp to complete is just the cherry on top. The biggest downsides of the doctrine though are its one-trick-poniness. While it is extremely effective at filling out a frontline with leg infantry and grinding potential offensives to a halt, it tends to fall short in other areas. It offers little to no bonuses for armored units, no notable offensive bonuses to any units besides ordinary infantry (who tend to perform poorly on the offense because of their low breakthrough), and no access to any powerful offensive tactics that other doctrines give.

Military theorists in the Soviet Union and Communist China were responsible for the development of groundbreaking military doctrines that would shape not only the second world war, but warfare in the 20th century in general. In the Soviet Union, military planners were pushed to come up with a new set of principles around which to organize their army that was a better fit for their state's new ideology compared to the doctrines of the old Russian Empire. What they came up with was a doctrine they called Deep Battle, which highlighted a new, operational level of thinking in between the tactical and strategic that would become a mainstay not just in the second world war, but 20th century warfare in general. Planners in Communist China were similarly faced with having to come up with a new doctrine that was a better fit for their ideology and situation, but the answer they came to was very different. Later called People's War, this doctrine combined elements of guerrilla warfare with a more conventional, structured approach that allowed an army to call upon guerrilla partisans when needed and still be able to act like a conventional army. Deployed successfully against Japan and later China, the doctrine would be adopted and used by many insurgent groups after the war.

Deep Battle, being a jack-of-all-trades that it is, can be a decent choice for a country that is not sure what it wants from its land doctrine and wants to be prepared for anything. It can also be useful for a country that wants to blend the bonuses of Grand Battleplan with a few key bonuses from other doctrines, e.g., access to Backhand Blow from Mobile Warfare and/or increased effective stats from Superior Firepower, though only to infantry .
Mass Mobilization can be a great choice for countries that wish to stall out enemy offensives, especially on minors who want to help hold a frontline while their majors focus on offensives. Even on major powers, its sheer attritional power can be combined with close air support to deal significant damage not just on the defense, but potentially even on the offense as well.

# Researching Doctrines [verified for 1.16]

Each individual grand doctrine and subdoctrine (excluding Great War Infantry at 50 army exp ) has a base cost of 100 army exp to unlock. This cost may be modified by spirits, advisors, and special bonuses from focuses, events, or decisions.

It is possible to change doctrines at any point, but all bonuses from previously unlocked doctrines are lost when unlocking a different doctrine and changing grand doctrines resets all tracks. Any doctrine-exclusive Officer Corps spirits are also automatically switched to different ones when a different doctrine tree is selected.

## Starting Doctrines
Some nations start the game with a land doctrine pre-selected and unlocked. They can still change doctrines as normal, though sticking with their starting doctrine can cost them less army exp in the long run.
