Constructions are fixed assets that may be built in states or provinces that are constructed using civilian factories.

# State buildings [verified for 1.16]

These buildings are built on a state level. Control of state resources or buildings requires controlling provinces worth at least 50% of the VP of that state, even if that does not include the province where the building icon is located. Airbases will appear, but do not become usable, unless the player also controls the province containing the airbase.

# Shared buildings [verified for 1.16]

Like state buildings, shared buildings are built on a state level; however, they share a limited number of slots. This is perhaps the most important category of constructions, since it contains the factories necessary for production.

Many of these buildings can be targeted by strategic bombing; if they receive even a single point of damage, the effects of the first level of the building are immediately disabled. This is especially effective against expensive buildings with only a single level, such as Stronghold Network , Nuclear Reactor , and Civilian Nuclear Reactor .

## Deconstructing
It is possible to deconstruct shared buildings from the state tab by hovering over the building icon and pressing the X that appears in the lower right corner of the icon. During wartime, each state can only deconstruct one building per 30 days.

# Civilian factories [verified for 1.16]

 Civilian factories make all the improvements to a territory and are necessary to build any of the constructions listed under the Construction tab . All nations start with a certain number of civilian factories and a percentage will be used for consumer goods, depending on the economy law and national spirits that are currently in place.

Civilian factories that are not used to produce consumer goods can be used for trade, to complete or repair the construction queued in the Construction tab, to spend on the international market, or to pursue decisions or intelligency agency investments. It's possible to increase the number of civilian factories by building more, but this is limited by the number of shared buildings slots available in each state

## Consumer goods
Depending on economic laws, national spirits, political advisors, and stability, a number of civilian factories will be occupied by producing consumer goods and are unavailable for construction, trade, or any other use of civilian factories. This number is computed as a percentage of the total number of factories, including military factories and factories received through trade, but not naval dockyards . Factories are counted even when they are damaged and can't produce anything. The number of consumer goods factories is always rounded down.

- Example
Suppose the Early Mobilization is enabled and no other modifiers apply, meaning that the final consumer goods need is 30%. A country has 50 military factories , 50 civilian factories , and 20 naval dockyards . It will then have to allocate 30 civilian factories to produce consumer goods, leaving it with 15 that it can use freely.

### Consumer goods factories factor
Consumer Goods Factories factor (CGFF) is an additional factor that is available in some countries, most notably Germany. It is the additional percentages of consumer goods in porportion to the current consumer goods factories. The number is multiplicative, not additive.

The following is the formula regarding CGFF:

Suppose a country with Early Mobilization , meaning a consumer goods need of 30%, and has +10% CGFF. It will have a final consumer goods need of 30% x (1+10%) = 33%, not 40%. This means if it had 100 total factories, 33 civilian factories will be allocated to producing consumer goods.

In some cases CGFF can go into the negatives. Similar what happens with positive CGFF, this will reduce the current consumer goods need multiplicatively. If the same example country with Early Mobilization had -10% CGFF instead, its final consumer goods need would be 30% x (1-10%) = 27%, not 20%.

Multiple sources of CGFF stack multiplicatively with each other. This means that multiple smaller sources of positive CGFF can have a greater effect than a single, larger source of positive CGFF. Inversely, a single large source of negative CGFF will have a greater effect than multiple smaller sources of negative CGFF.

## Efficiency of additional civilian factories

The return on investment of building an additional Civilian Factory will depend on the Construction Speed bonus as well the Economy Law currently in place, or expected in the future. The higher the Construction Speed bonus is, the more Output will be done by the newly built Civilian Factory and the faster it will accumulate an Output equal to its cost, 10800. This variable is the time, in days, required to recover the cost of building the factory, or payback time.

A country's economy law will, in addition to sometimes affecting civilian factory construction speed, also dictate what ratio of civilian factories are used for fulfilling consumer goods needs vs. for free use as output, trade, etc. The higher this ratio is, the longer the payback time of newly built civilian factories will be, as there is a greater likelihood that a newly built civilian factory will be allocated to producing consumer goods instead of having useful output. # Infrastructure level [verified for ?]

Construction speed of all buildings depends on the level of infrastructure in the state. Construction speed is multiplied by
-

Where the INFRA_MAX_CONSTRUCTION_COST_EFFECT in defines defaults to 1, and Maximum Infrastructure Level defaults to 5 in common/buildings/00_buildings.txt.

For example, at 3/5 infrastructure in a state, construction speed in that state is multiplied by 1.6x. In the Construction UI, this bonus is displayed for each State as a colored percentage next to an Infrastructure road icon. This bonus is applied after bonuses from tech, advisors, and national spirits, making 1 level of infrastructure a more powerful bonus than, e.g., a War Industrialist.

It is usually recommended to allocate an infrastructure/civilian factory building phase for your nation before subsequently militarizing by building military factories and naval dockyards. However, the scope of civilian factory building can be reduced substantially if early warfare is planned or expected, and/or if newly built dockyards are meant to produce ships with very long production times.

Due to the construction boost from infrastructure, the most efficient method involves filling all building slots one state at a time. This can be done by prioritizing states with higher starting infrastructure levels. The mechanics of the game encourages players to concentrate industry in central, high-infrastructure areas, although this must be balanced against the increased vulnerability of having all of one's industry in one place. It is common for all building slots to be filled before the game ends, this represents the in-game factory cap for the respective territory.

# Factory conversion [verified for 1.16]

 Civilian factories can be converted into military factories for base cost of 4000, and military factories can be converted into civilian factories for base cost of 9000. The base cost is modified by economy laws and can be futher modified by national spirits and political advisors. To convert a factory, simply click on the corresponding state and hover over the factory that is to be converted. Alternatiely, use the convert button in the construction tab, and conversions will appear in the tab, just like any other building project.

# Damage and repair [verified for 1.9]

Each building level has 100 HP. They may get damaged by port strikes (naval bases), land combat (infrastructure, forts), resistance (factories), strategic bombing, or explicit events (like the Soviet Union's decision to move factories to the Urals). Nuclear bombs can destroy multiple or all levels of a building at once, for details see nuclear bomb effects.

The damage from land combat (against infrastructure) and strategic bombing gets scaled by the building's damage factor (air base and radar: 0.5, anti-air: 0.1, 1 otherwise) and the squared ratio of the currently undamaged building levels to the maximum level. For example, a level-3 anti-air building (maximum level 5) where one level is already partially damaged only receives of incoming damage from strategic bombing.

A single military factory in a state (or in other words a level-1 military factory) receives while 15 military factories receive . High-level factories and infrastructure will generally get damaged much more quickly from these two damage sources than lower levels.

Anti-air provides damage resistance from bomber-type attacks (port strikes and strategic bombing) depending on the level of anti-air in the containing state and researched anti-air technologies. Dispersed Industry provides an alternative source of damage resistance but only for factories and not stacking with anti-air. The maximum of the two is used for factories.

All damaged buildings appear in the construction list. The speed of factories assigned to a repair line gets a bonus of +200% plus any Factory Repair Speed bonuses (e.g., from construction technologies or the Construction Repair continuous focus , infrastructure levels). Even when no factories are assigned, the speed of the line is 1.

The resulting daily repair progress is , rounded to the nearest full percent. So when no factories are assigned, repairs will still progress by 1% per day. Assigning just a few factories to repair an expensive building may also produce just 1% progress per day, effectively wasting the output of those factories.
