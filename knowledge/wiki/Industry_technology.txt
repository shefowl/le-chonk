Industry technology unlocks technologies related to industrial output, construction speed, repair speed, resource gain efficiency, fuel gain efficiency, and the construction of fuel infrastructure and synthetic refineries. Like engineering technologies, industry technologies tend to provide general bonuses on the country level, but unlike engineering technologies, these bonuses are all strictly related to economic bonuses, both direct and indirect.

## Concentrated vs. Dispersed industry [verified for 1.16]

Both the concentrated or dispersed industry branches have their advantages and drawbacks, and the choice for which one to use depends on the player's play style. Should situations change in-game, players can also always switch from one branch to another, though doing so will force them to have to research all of the technologies in that branch from scratch.

- Concentrated industry
- Has 5 levels that can be researched which span from 1936 to 1943. Each new level provides the same bonus as previous levels. By choosing Concentrated Industry , the player increases the output of their military factories more than what they would get by choosing Dispersed Industry . This allows the player to make both new and existing military factories more efficient, though only if they are assigned to producing the same equipment for a while.

- Dispersed industry
- Has 5 levels that can be researched which span from 1936 and 1943. Each new level provides the same bonuses besides the first one, which provides slightly more Factory bomb vulnerability reduction than the rest. By choosing Dispersed Industry , the player reduces the vulnerability of their factories to strategic bombing, as well as increasing the base production efficiency of all of their production lines and the retention of said efficiency compared to what they would get by choosing Concentrated Industry . This reduces the player's susceptibility to strategic bombing, makes newly built military factories more efficient for the first couple of months of production, and allows the player more leeway in reacting to changes in production needs, including when switching them to upgraded versions of newly outdated equipment.

One can think of the two styles as maximizing potential production at any given production efficiency vs. decreasing time to achieve 100% production efficiency. In general, the concentrated option produces more output after about a year to a year-and-a-half producing the same equipment on the same production line, while the dispersed option produces more output in the first year to year-and-a-half of a new production line and the first two to three years for an existing production line that has been switched to produce an upgraded version of the same equipment type.
